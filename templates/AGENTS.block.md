## agentic-workflow ({project})

This project runs changes through agentic-workflow. The `wf` CLI enforces the rules below; when it refuses, fix the cause instead of working around it.

**Lifecycle.** `wf entry` → `wf plan` (criteria frozen before code, with the plan's own prediction `expect`) → `wf handoff implementer` → commit → code review (`wf handoff reviewer`, a different agent each round, `wf review`; the first review audits the whole change, later rounds review what changed since) until a round comes back clean → ONE `wf gate` on that tree → evidence review by a new reviewer (a full audit the first time, a delta after a fix) → `wf accept` → `wf deliver` → show every delivered screenshot to the user in the chat with its caption and `wf shown` → tracker handoff (screenshots uploaded as files) with `wf tracker record`. `wf resume` always says what is next.

**Rules**

- Work only in the attempt's worktrees under `.wf-worktrees/`, never in the main checkouts.
- Review and gate never run side by side: no gate until a code-review round on the tree comes back clean, and no reviewer handoff while a gate runs (`wf` refuses both; only the user skips the order: `wf gate --reason "<why>"` opens the decision, and they confirm it as every reserved decision is confirmed, below). The one exception needs no approval: when an evidence review's blocking findings were fixed by the implementers handed after it (each closed, the tree is what they left, and no scope was added since), `wf gate` runs and the evidence review after it verifies the fix. Never edit the worktrees while a gate runs (`wf status` shows it); a stop needs its class (`wf stop --class major-finding|tree-change|owner-decision --reason "<why>"`). A `--focused` gate is repair proof only; acceptance and delivery need a gate without it.
- Report a step as done only after `wf` accepted it. Gate results, reviews and deliveries are proven by files under `.wf-evidence/`, which no one edits by hand.
- The reviewer never planned, wrote or owns the change.
- Agents predict and the owner agent judges: the plan states what it expects (files, review rounds, minutes), an implementer its approach and minutes, a reviewer its scope and minutes, and `wf resume`, `wf status` and `wf signals` show each beside what happened, with no limit. At each round boundary (a recorded review, a closed implementer) the owner agent compares them; when the course diverges it hands the strategist (`wf handoff strategist --agent <new id> --reason "<what diverged>"`, then waits for its completion notification or runs it in the foreground) or re-plans, and records why. Never poll a running agent. The strategist may contract the work (consolidate, split into sibling attempts in the same batch, delta review, ticket observations); it never waives a finding that blocks. The engine stops only for the user's own round escalation (`wf approve`) and a strategist's escalation.
- One severity scale for every reviewer and implementer: **high** = a user, a tenant or the business is harmed or blocked (wrong or lost data, a broken flow, security, money, a visible defect on a primary screen); **medium** = degraded but usable (a secondary flow, an edge case, a confusing state); **low** = polish (wording, spacing, naming, style). A tenant-isolation, authorization or money defect is always high.
- A finding blocks a round only when it fails a frozen criterion, breaks a critical invariant or regresses a line the change touched (since the last reviewed tree, in a delta round), and is rated medium or high, or not rated (`wf` computes this); a finding rated low is an observation in every round (one that fails a criterion or breaks an invariant is high whatever its rating), and a full-round finding that cites nothing checkable blocks until a later round locates or verifies it. Every other finding is an observation, and delivery waits until it is placed: fixed (verified by the next reviewer, or `wf scope fixed` with a commit made after the finding; a later reviewer's `open` undoes it, and a high one still needs a reviewer's `fixed`), moved to a sibling attempt (`wf scope move`), ticketed (`wf scope ticket`) or left for later as a known issue (`wf scope defer`); `wf resume` names the open ones before the gate. What may leave the delivery follows the user's severity rule, for review findings and discovered issues alike: high (or unrated) is fixed in this delivery and leaves only with the user's `wf approve`; medium is your judgement, with the reason recorded; low may be ticketed. A finding that blocks leaves only by the user's `wf approve`, whatever its severity, as waiving it does (`wf scope waive`). Never increase scope silently.
- A user's "hold", "local only" or "don't push" is recorded at once with `wf hold --reason "<their words>"`; only they lift it: `wf release` opens the decision. Every reserved decision (waiving, ticketing or deferring a blocking finding, overriding the gate order, a high-severity defect leaving the delivery, lifting a hold, adopting a live owner's attempt, the round escalation) is the user's: they type `wf approve <id>` in their own terminal, or you ask them in your question panel with the exact question and options the refused command printed and then run `wf approve <id> --from-question` (the answer the harness recorded is theirs). Never a flag or a phrase an agent passes, and never your own reading of their chat words (docs/trust-model.md).
- Never read, print or move secret values. Missing secrets are entered by the user with `wf secrets guide` in their own terminal.
- Tracker comments describe what to test in product language: no file paths, commits, hashes or test counts.
- Never set a ticket to done; a human does.
- Prior decisions (an earlier merge, an existing test, a prior verdict) are inputs, not authority: justify kept behaviour from who uses the surface and what they need.
- Lessons the project learned live in each repo's `.workflow/lessons/` (cross-repo ones in the adapter repo; `wf lesson list`). When the user corrects you or a ticket is reopened, record one in the repo it concerns with `wf lesson add --repo <name>` (committed and delivered with the attempt); a finding about the workflow itself is a plugin improvement (`wf improve add`), never a lesson.
