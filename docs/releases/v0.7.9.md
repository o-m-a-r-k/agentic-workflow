## 0.7.9 - The workflow learns a project by working in it

- D19: one project had 70 lessons, all `proposed`, 16 recurred and none ever curated. Recurrences were counted only
  when a reviewer volunteered a verdict, and merging, promoting and retiring lessons waited for a person. The lessons
  were narrow (one per ticket), so a repeated mistake was spread over near-copies, and every lesson that had recurred
  once passed the bundle cap. Learning is now automatic, and it changes only what roles read and in which order.
  - **At review acceptance** a classifier labels what the attempt taught:
    - each finding any round raised: which lesson's mistake it repeats, or none;
    - each new or never-compared lesson: which other lesson describes the same mistake, or none.

    The classifier is a cheap model given fixed choices, with no tools and no reasoning: Claude `haiku` and Codex
    `gpt-6-luna` by default. A project pins its own per runtime in the adapter's `learning.classifier`, or turns it off
    with `learning: { enabled: false }`; `WF_LEARNING=off` turns it off on one machine. `wf review accept` says what it
    learned. A failed classifier is one line in `wf status` and never fails an acceptance.
  - **Recurrence and ranking.** A repeat counts as the lesson's recurrence, once per lesson per attempt. Bundles rank
    lessons by evidence: most recent repeat, then most repeats, then carried in the fewest attempts. Only enforced
    lessons pass the cap now.
  - **Merges.** `wf resume` names lessons that describe one mistake where they can be merged (an open attempt that holds
    their repos, before its review is accepted), and the owner merges them there:
    `wf lesson merge <ids> --title "<the one mistake>" --attempt <id>`. The merge is committed, reviewed and delivered
    with the attempt; the members are retired with `mergedInto`, and the merged lesson keeps their ranking evidence. A
    merge one attempt made is not proposed to another. `wf lesson distinct <ids>` tells the workflow the classifier was
    wrong: no merge of them is proposed again. A lesson recorded from a finding is never counted as that finding's repeat.
  - **What the person chose.** Items the person chose to fix when asked whether they could leave (`fix now`, or their
    own words) reach later reviewers as `severityScale.personFixed` (the 20 most recent): examples to rate by, never a
    rule.
  - **Estimates.** Each delivered attempt's prediction beside what happened reaches later planners and strategists as
    `pastEstimates`, per lane. It never reaches an implementer or a reviewer (D9).
  - **Where it lives.** The measurement is in the project's git-excluded `.workflow-maintenance/learned.jsonl`; lesson
    text stays in the repositories.
  - **Limits.** Nothing learned asks the person, stops, refuses, lowers a rating or changes who lets an item leave.
    A change from the improvement inbox now passes an experience review before it ships (CONTRIBUTING).
- I-130: a lesson recurrence found at or after acceptance wrote the lesson file in the pull-only main checkout. A
  recurrence is no longer written into any lesson file: the file keeps the count it was committed with, and
  `wf lesson list` shows the learned count beside it.
- I-131: scenario runs wrote refusals from temporary projects into the person's own refusal log (138 of 335 entries in
  one log), which `wf refusals` and plugin triage then read as real use. Every scenario process now keeps its user-level
  state (refusal log, inbox, registry, leases) in its own temporary folder.
- I-127: after an analysis attempt was upgraded to implementation, fresh planners got the analysis plan and no word of
  why it changed. They could only amend criteria, and an amendment that carried only `plan.summary` was accepted and
  then dropped.
  - Planners now see the attempt's `intent` and its `intentChanges` with the reason.
  - Until the first implementer is handed, `wf plan` replaces the analysis plan whole (`replacesPlan` tells the planner).
  - `wf handoff planner --context "<text>"` (or `--context-file`) carries the person's words to the planner, recorded
    with the handoff.
  - An amendment field no amendment applies is refused rather than silently dropped when it would be lost (a plan file
    sent again with its plan and survey as frozen is still accepted; attempts admitted from 0.7.9).
- I-129: the outside-plan list ignored anchors written `<repo>:<path>`, and the files an accepted impact amendment's
  survey brought into scope. Both now count, in the owner's warning, the reviewer's bundle, the signals and the
  strategist's plan files (the plan's own survey lists what the change reaches, so it still does not count as planned).
- I-128: `wf handoff stop` recorded a role as stopped while its native process tree kept running.
  - The stop now ends the whole tree: the role's process group, every descendant, and children that left the group.
    It escalates to SIGKILL, and records `stopped` only once every process is observed gone.
  - When something cannot be signalled, it says so, names the processes and records nothing.
  - A recorded pid the system has given to another process since (it started well after the launch) is left alone: the
    launch is recorded stopped and nothing is signalled. A pid seen in the tree that moved to another group is not
    ended either.
  - An interrupted foreground `wf handoff run` ends its tree the same way, and reports a launch it could not end as
    still running.
