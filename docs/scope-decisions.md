# What blocks, and where the rest goes

**The engine decides what blocks, from facts it can check.** Reviewers find about two issues per hundred tool calls whatever the model, so a finding count measures effort, not defects. A finding blocks the gate and acceptance only when it (a) cites a frozen criterion the change fails (`criterion: "C2"`), (b) breaks a critical invariant (`invariant: "<which one>"`), or (c) is a regression on a line this attempt changed (the engine compares the finding's `path:line`, from `file` and `line` or from `evidence`, with the attempt's diff against its base; in a delta round, with the lines changed since the last reviewed tree), and it is rated medium or high, or not rated. No one declares causality: it is computed. A finding rated low never opens a round, in any round: it is an observation (a finding that fails a criterion or breaks an invariant is high whatever its rating). A full-round finding that cites no location, criterion or invariant cannot be checked, so it blocks until a later round locates it or verifies it. A later reviewer verifying a finding still open may re-rate it (`priorFindings: [{ round, id, status, evidence, severity }]`); re-rated low it stops opening rounds, but where it may go keeps the higher of the two ratings.

**Severity is one scale for every reviewer and implementer.** **High** = a user, a tenant or the business is harmed or blocked (wrong or lost data, a broken flow, security, money, a visible defect on a primary screen). **Medium** = degraded but usable (a secondary flow, an edge case, a confusing state). **Low** = polish (wording, spacing, naming, style). A tenant-isolation, authorization or money defect is always high.

**Observations.** Every other open finding is an **observation**. An observation never opens a round; it ends fixed (a later round verifies it, or `wf scope fixed --finding <reviewer:F1> --commit <sha>` records a commit made after the finding was raised: a later reviewer's `open` verdict undoes it, and a high observation settled this way still needs a reviewer's `fixed` before delivery), ticketed (`wf scope ticket --finding <reviewer:F1>[,<reviewer:F2>] --capture <raw readback>`), moved to a sibling attempt of the delivery batch (`wf scope move --finding <reviewer:F1> --sibling <attempt>`) or left for later as a known issue listed in the delivered comment (`wf scope defer --finding <reviewer:F1>[,<reviewer:F2>] --reason "..."`), and `wf deliver` refuses while one is unresolved. An observation, like an open discovered issue, joins the fix round that happens anyway (`alsoFix` in its bundle), or, when nothing blocks, one planned pass before the gate: an amendment's `pass: [{ items, why, repos }]` groups every outstanding item into work items that name them and no criterion, one implementer builds it, and the gate runs with no code review of its own while the evidence review after it verifies the pass (0.7.28, D45; `wf resume` says `pass`). The person's "fix all" is recorded once with `wf scope fix-all --person-said "<their words>"`, the words checked to be verbatim in one of their own messages in the session, written since the attempt was admitted or in the message that started it or one that names its item, never one that, without naming this ticket, replies to another ticket's question or names other tickets of the project (or the person runs it in their own terminal and types yes): no item is asked about leaving again, whoever the policy lets decide it, unless they direct one out in new words. `wf scope list` shows every open finding, whether it blocks, its family and where it went.

**Placement is not obligation.** Nothing broken is dropped, but where a fix lives can change. A move to a sibling attempt of the batch asks nobody, because the obligation stays in this delivery; a finding on a line this attempt changed is fixed here, since a sibling starts from the base and has no such line. Once a member's batch is assembled (its HEAD is pinned, and a commit on it would be refused at delivery), the batch itself is the place an open observation goes: `wf scope move --finding <ref> --sibling <batch id>` gives the batch an open discovered issue that names where it came from, the batch's implementer and reviewer fix it and `wf discovered close <id> --fixed <batch commit>` closes it, and the member keeps the HEAD it was accepted at. The line rule does not apply there, because the batch contains the member's lines (0.7.23, I-177). Ejecting a member rebuilds the batch from its base and the other members, so it is refused while the batch holds commits of its own (`--drop-own-commits` takes them out knowingly and records them). What may leave the delivery follows the person's rule (D17): medium and high are fixed in this delivery, a low one leaves only with the person's answer, one short question per item (D18; the project's `leaving:` may change who decides), every item named together asked in the same panel (`wf scope defer --finding <ref>,<D-id>`, or `wf scope ticket`), kind `move-out-of-batch`, key `leave:<ref>`, choices `let it leave` | `fix now`; an answer in the person's own words is recorded (`other`) and followed; medium and high are refused (the person's `deliver-core` answer to the round escalation is the one exception). A finding that blocks never leaves: the person may waive it (`wf scope waive`). The person's `deliver-core` answer to the round escalation covers only the findings and discovered issues recorded before it. The strategist schedules observations: a batch fix pass, a sibling attempt, or a ticket.

**Families.** Findings that share a defect pattern carry one `family` key. A repair handoff lists the blocking findings by family, and each family is answered once by one mechanical proof (in the implementer's report, or a commit trailer, `Sweep <id>: proof - <command> => <expected result>`: a query, lint rule or test and what it returns once the family is clean); the reviewer re-runs the proof. There is no answer per hit, and a sweep query from the owner (`--sweep`) is optional. A family is the reviewer's label for a symptom; findings that share a cause, wherever they show, are linked by the reviewer (`sameCauseAs`) and a fix round's goal is that no review finds the problem again anywhere (0.7.27, I-191).

**Discovered issues.** Record a defect you find with `wf discovered add --summary "..." --where <file:line> --severity high|medium|low` (and `--invariant "<which>"` when it breaks a critical invariant, which makes it high; `wf discovered rate <id> --severity <s> --reason "..."` re-rates one: raising is free, lowering records its reason and, out of high, needs the person's `wf approve`); fix a related one in this attempt, record an unrelated one without repairing it, and amend the criteria when a related consumer is missing (no cosmetic workaround). Closing one as deferred follows the same rule; `wf scope defer --finding D1,D2` (or `wf discovered close D1,D2 --deferred`) puts several to the person together, one short question per item. One that turns out never to have been a defect is closed with `wf discovered close D1 --not-a-defect --reason "<why>"` (the person decides, one question per item; an independent reviewer's `verified-nonissue` verdict in its closure closes it too): the claim stays in the record and the delivered comment does not list it. Your own lint, formatting or fixture fixes are bookkeeping: `--kind bookkeeping`, closed with the fix commit in the same handoff, never handed to a reviewer. Required gate failures stay failures.

The owning chat routes repair findings to their implementer; never fix them yourself. If another work item owns the file, record the discovered issue with `--blocked-by <work item>` and route it to that implementer.

**The person's own disposition waives the findings that repeat it (0.7.33, I-228, D85).** When the person already said, for this ticket, what happens to an item (they handed a production change to the client and said to stop reporting it), the findings later rounds raise about it are not asked again: `wf scope waive --finding <ref>[,<ref>...] --person-said "<their exact words>" --reason "<how they cover these findings>"` records each waiver with their words (`personSaid`), once the engine has found the words verbatim in one of their own messages in the session, about this ticket (the same proof as `wf scope fix-all` and `wf decide`). That the words cover these findings is the agent's reading, stated in its reason; each finding keeps its severity and history, and the delivered comment says it was accepted at the person's direction, in their words. In the person's own terminal there is nothing to prove: they answer the question. Discovered issues keep their own route (`wf discovered close --person-directed`, one question each).
