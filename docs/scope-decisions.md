# What blocks, and where the rest goes

**The engine decides what blocks, from facts it can check.** Reviewers find about two issues per hundred tool calls whatever the model, so a finding count measures effort, not defects. A finding blocks the gate and acceptance only when it (a) cites a frozen criterion the change fails (`criterion: "C2"`), (b) breaks a critical invariant (`invariant: "<which one>"`), or (c) is a regression on a line this attempt changed (the engine compares the finding's `path:line`, from `file` and `line` or from `evidence`, with the attempt's diff against its base; in a delta round, with the lines changed since the last reviewed tree), and it is rated medium or high, or not rated. No one declares causality: it is computed. A finding rated low never opens a round, in any round: it is an observation (a finding that fails a criterion or breaks an invariant is high whatever its rating). A full-round finding that cites no location, criterion or invariant cannot be checked, so it blocks until a later round locates it or verifies it. A later reviewer verifying a finding still open may re-rate it (`priorFindings: [{ round, id, status, evidence, severity }]`); re-rated low it stops opening rounds, but where it may go keeps the higher of the two ratings.

**Severity is one scale for every reviewer and implementer.** **High** = a user, a tenant or the business is harmed or blocked (wrong or lost data, a broken flow, security, money, a visible defect on a primary screen). **Medium** = degraded but usable (a secondary flow, an edge case, a confusing state). **Low** = polish (wording, spacing, naming, style). A tenant-isolation, authorization or money defect is always high.

**Observations.** Every other open finding is an **observation**. An observation never opens a round; it ends fixed (a later round verifies it, or `wf scope fixed --finding <reviewer:F1> --commit <sha>` records a commit made after the finding was raised: a later reviewer's `open` verdict undoes it, and a high observation settled this way still needs a reviewer's `fixed` before delivery), ticketed (`wf scope ticket --finding <reviewer:F1>[,<reviewer:F2>] --capture <raw readback>`), moved to a sibling attempt of the delivery batch (`wf scope move --finding <reviewer:F1> --sibling <attempt>`) or left for later as a known issue listed in the delivered comment (`wf scope defer --finding <reviewer:F1>[,<reviewer:F2>] --reason "..."`), and `wf deliver` refuses while one is unresolved. Before the gate, `wf resume` names the open observations so they are placed now. `wf scope list` shows every open finding, whether it blocks, its family and where it went.

**Placement is not obligation.** Nothing broken is dropped, but where a fix lives can change. A move to a sibling attempt of the batch asks nobody, because the obligation stays in this delivery; a finding on a line this attempt changed is fixed here, since a sibling starts from the base and has no such line. Once a member's batch is assembled (its HEAD is pinned, and a commit on it would be refused at delivery), the batch itself is the place an open observation goes: `wf scope move --finding <ref> --sibling <batch id>` gives the batch an open discovered issue that names where it came from, the batch's implementer and reviewer fix it and `wf discovered close <id> --fixed <batch commit>` closes it, and the member keeps the HEAD it was accepted at. The line rule does not apply there, because the batch contains the member's lines (0.7.23, I-177). Ejecting a member rebuilds the batch from its base and the other members, so it is refused while the batch holds commits of its own (`--drop-own-commits` takes them out knowingly and records them). What may leave the delivery follows the person's rule (D17): medium and high are fixed in this delivery, a low one leaves only with the person's answer, one short question per item (D18; the project's `leaving:` may change who decides), every item named together asked in the same panel (`wf scope defer --finding <ref>,<D-id>`, or `wf scope ticket`), kind `move-out-of-batch`, key `leave:<ref>`, choices `let it leave` | `fix now`; an answer in the person's own words is recorded (`other`) and followed; medium and high are refused (the person's `deliver-core` answer to the round escalation is the one exception). A finding that blocks never leaves: the person may waive it (`wf scope waive`). The person's `deliver-core` answer to the round escalation covers only the findings and discovered issues recorded before it. The strategist schedules observations: a batch fix pass, a sibling attempt, or a ticket.

**Families.** Findings that share a defect pattern carry one `family` key. A repair handoff lists the blocking findings by family, and each family is answered once by one mechanical proof (in the implementer's report, or a commit trailer, `Sweep <id>: proof - <command> => <expected result>`: a query, lint rule or test and what it returns once the family is clean); the reviewer re-runs the proof. There is no answer per hit, and a sweep query from the owner (`--sweep`) is optional.

**Discovered issues.** Record a defect you find with `wf discovered add --summary "..." --where <file:line> --severity high|medium|low` (and `--invariant "<which>"` when it breaks a critical invariant, which makes it high; `wf discovered rate <id> --severity <s> --reason "..."` re-rates one: raising is free, lowering records its reason and, out of high, needs the person's `wf approve`); fix a related one in this attempt, record an unrelated one without repairing it, and amend the criteria when a related consumer is missing (no cosmetic workaround). Closing one as deferred follows the same rule; `wf scope defer --finding D1,D2` (or `wf discovered close D1,D2 --deferred`) puts several to the person together, one short question per item. Your own lint, formatting or fixture fixes are bookkeeping: `--kind bookkeeping`, closed with the fix commit in the same handoff, never handed to a reviewer. Required gate failures stay failures.

The owning chat routes repair findings to their implementer; never fix them yourself. If another work item owns the file, record the discovered issue with `--blocked-by <work item>` and route it to that implementer.
