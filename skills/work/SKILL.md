# Work a ticket

Check `wf status --quiet` exits 0; otherwise this project is not onboarded (offer the onboard skill).

The user's request to implement a ticket authorizes the whole lifecycle below, delivery included, unless they say otherwise. If they say hold, local only or don't push, run `wf hold --reason "<their words>"` at once.

`wf resume` always names the next step. With more than one open attempt, pass `--attempt <id>`; the commands `wf` prints already include it.

## The goal: finish, not just keep going

The plan predicts how long the ticket takes, and you judge whether it is on course, not the engine. Agents predict: the planner's plan states what it expects (`plan.expect`: files, review rounds, minutes to delivery), each implementer its approach and expected minutes in its report file (written early, completed at the end), each reviewer its scope and expected minutes in its closure, and the strategist its own prediction. `wf resume`, `wf status` and `wf signals --attempt <id>` show every one beside what happened (changed files, rounds, minutes since admission, each running role's elapsed time and last activity against its own estimate). No number in the engine stops a handoff or a role; reading the comparison is your job, and when an actual passes its own role's estimate the `next:` line of `wf resume` says so first. An estimate is a prediction to judge when it is written, not only when it is passed: one far from the size of the work handed (a long estimate for small fixes) is a question about the role's approach, asked then. When you report time, say how long a role ran apart from how long it waited on you, quote a role's expectation only from its own report (`plan.expect.minutes` is the whole delivery's), and give no duration for a fix whose cause is not yet found.

**At each round boundary** (a review round recorded, an implementer closed, before every repair round) look at plan against actual and analyze: what explains the difference (the cause and the class of the problem, not the one instance), and is the approach converging? Signs it is not: findings across rounds share a cause (a round finds the next place one wrong assumption lives: reviewers link them with `sameCauseAs`, and `wf resume` and `wf signals` say "the cause of X came back in Y" when a cause returns after a fix, or "has the family of X, which was marked fixed" when only the family matches: check whether they share a cause; stop the one-place fixes, then find the root cause with an analysis or the strategist's `cluster` before the next fix round), the blocking findings did not go down since the last round (read `blockingOpen` in `wf signals`, which counts every round's: a round that raised nothing new has cleared nothing an earlier round raised, and `wf resume` lists the ones carried), each fix widens the diff, implementers keep discovering consumers one at a time, a role is far past its own estimate with no recent activity (look at it; a stalled one is stopped and re-handed, never polled), or busy with runs that cannot add information (repeating what the gate will run; steady commits are not progress when the runs between them prove nothing new). When the course diverged and you cannot explain it away, change the approach instead of repeating it: hand the strategist (below) with what you saw as its `--reason`, or re-plan (`wf criteria amend`, whose file may carry the new `expect`, and whose `--reason` is the record). A difference you can explain, in a ticket that is still converging, needs neither; say it in your next report to the user.

What stays a hard stop: at 10 review rounds (handed, on every attempt being worked, whichever release admitted it) the decision is the user's: bring them the brief from `wf signals` (the numbers, what keeps coming back, 2-3 options and your recommendation); the next handoff opens the decision they answer with `wf approve` (continue for another 10; split: unstarted work items, observations and discovered issues move to a sibling attempt with `wf split`, code already built stays; deliver-core: what the criteria need and what blocks stays, and every other finding or discovered issue recorded before the answer may be ticketed under that one decision, never one raised after it (the one way a medium or high item leaves); or stop). Handed rounds count, recorded or not. A strategist's escalation awaits `wf approve` too, and two missed strategist predictions in a row make its next decision an escalation. If you withdraw an escalation because its question was wrong (`wf decisions withdraw`), hand the strategist again with what changed and record its corrected decision: a recorded decision supersedes the withdrawn question (until then the next handoff asks it again, as it was), and `wf resume` says which step is next. The person's answer in their own words is followed, or withdrawn with what they said, before a new decision is recorded.

## Give every agent a goal, not a method

Each role gets the goal of its step and what good looks like; the engine checks the facts at the boundaries. Never brief an agent with how to do its work, which files to stay in, or which tests to skip, and never steer a reviewer. What you add to a role's printed start line, if anything, is context it lacks (for a fix round: the cause you found), never instructions.

| Step | The role's goal | Checked by |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Plan | targets an implementer can build and a person can check, with the quality bar turned into targets for this ticket | the engine re-runs the planner's queries |
| Implement | its criteria hold on the whole combined tree and nothing it touched regresses; it has reviewed its own diff against the criteria, the quality bar and the invariants before it reports | `wf check` on the combined tree, the implementer's report, the review |
| Review | the truth about whether the change meets its goals and what it broke | the engine decides what blocks |
| Strategy | the attempt finishes at the lowest cost that keeps its guarantees | its prediction, shown against what happens |

## Analyze before deciding

When the user, a finding, a failing gate or a discovery would change scope or approach, stop before acting: find the cause and the class of the problem (not the one instance), state the options with their cost and risk, pick one with a reason, and record it (the amendment's `--reason`, or a strategy decision). An aside from the user is a direction to analyze, not a command to apply within seconds; when it widens scope, tell them what it costs (rounds, time, a sibling attempt) before you act on it. Take the person's scope words literally: deciding means choosing inside what they asked, never narrowing it.

## Roles

Role agents come from `wf sync`: `wf-planner`, `wf-implementer` (and `wf-implementer-<class>`), `wf-reviewer`, `wf-strategist`. Start the agent type a handoff names, under the name it prints (`name it <id>`), on the bundle path it prints, and nothing else: never `general-purpose`, never a `model` override when you start it. If your harness cannot start an agent type by name, launch every role with `wf handoff run --agent <id> --attempt <id>` (native launches load fresh engine instructions, the frozen model and effort, and record the session id); a failed or finished launch is never reused: create a fresh handoff. A role file changed by a plugin update after this session started is not what an agent you start by type loads: its bundle then points it at the current file, which it reads first, so no restart is needed.

**Never poll.** Run a role in the foreground (`wf handoff run` waits for it, and hands control back once the role passes its own estimate while it keeps running: then judge it by what it is doing now, not by whether it is active (read its report and what it is running: a run the gate will make anyway, such as a whole spec or suite, or a run under load it added itself, adds nothing, so tell it to stop that, report and close), `wf handoff wait --agent <id> [--minutes N]` to follow it again (to its end, or N more minutes from now), or `wf handoff stop --agent <id>` and a fresh handoff; a role that states no estimate of its own, such as the planner or the strategist, gets yours at launch: `wf handoff run --agent <id> --minutes N`) or wait for the host's completion notification. No `sleep` loops, Monitors or repeated `wf status` while a role works. `wf signals` shows each working role's last transcript activity beside its own estimate; look at that once at a round boundary, not in a loop.

**A role that finishes must reach you.** On a harness that tells you when a role or background command ends (Claude Code does), ending your turn to wait for that notification is fine. On one that does not (Codex never starts a turn when a role or launch finishes), your turn does not end while a role you handed is running: a turn that ends with a status line leaves the result unread for hours (one owner sat 192 minutes after its implementer finished). Launch with `wf handoff run --agent <id> --minutes N` and wait in your turn with `wf handoff wait --agent <id> --minutes N`, N within what your harness allows one call to take, and call it again when it returns (or use your harness's own wait on the agent), and end the turn only to hand back to the user: a question asked in your message as `wf` printed it (the panel too, if you like), a finished delivery, or their stop. A decision you need from the user is asked in your message as `wf` printed it (or in the question panel), never only described in prose. Whenever your turn starts again, whatever started it (the user, a compaction, a wake), run `wf resume` first and act on every role that finished before you answer anything else.

## The loop

1. **Admit.** `wf entry --item <ID>` (`<ID>` is the tracker's ticket id such as `ENG-12`, never a page or database UUID; a project with another id shape sets `tracker.itemPattern`; `--intent analysis` for investigate-only; an analysis attempt can later become an implementation with `wf intent implementation --attempt <id> --reason "<the user's request>"`, then hand a fresh planner, whose plan replaces the analysis one with `wf plan` until the first implementer; with no tracker, save the request to a file and pass `--issue-file <file>`). Perform the printed tracker actions, then record the RAW tracker response (Linear `get_issue`, whole JSON unchanged) with `wf tracker record --event admitted --capture <file>`.
2. **Plan.** `wf handoff planner --agent <id>` (`--context "<what the user asked>"` carries the user's words or a decision to the planner); start `wf-planner` on the bundle. When it reports, `wf plan --from-agent <id>` (never retype its YAML; on another runtime save its reply unchanged and use `wf plan --file`). A project plans with a panel by default (0.7.22): two planners (on Claude Code Fable and Opus, on Codex Astra twice, as the project's classes pin them) each draft the whole plan blind and at the same time, the first seat merges the drafts and names every point they differ on, and the other seat agrees or objects until it agrees; its agreement freezes the plan. Each step is a `wf handoff planner` (the handoff prints the seat, the stage and the agent type, such as `wf-planner-review`) and a `wf plan --from-agent <id>`; `wf resume` says which step is next. Run the two drafts at the same time (an engine-launched harness: `wf handoff run --agent <id> --detach` for each, then `wf handoff wait --agent <id>`); continue a seat's own planner for its merge or confirmation when your harness can. For cross-cutting work the plan carries the survey and impact map with queries; `wf plan` re-runs the queries and refuses mismatched counts: send the refusal back to the same planner (a fresh handoff for an engine-launched planner), never fix counts yourself (a refused or unreadable plan is kept and handed to the next planner in `priorPlanRefusals`). Read the impact map before freezing. A ticket that sweeps many modules (for example "every table") is planned as module batches: separate work items, or sibling attempts in one batch (`wf split`), not one giant diff. In an investigation, query the read-only sources (logs, database, cluster, tracker) before asking the user for a fact. A criterion that needs screenshots needs a route to them: a gate step that collects captures, or, where the project has none, captures taken on the reviewed tree and brought in with `wf captures import` (`wf plan` warns when neither exists).
3. **Implement.** `wf handoff implementer --agent <id>`: an implementer handed without `--work` covers every work item no implementer has started, so no plan amendment is needed for one implementer to build several; `--work <W>` hands one item, for items built apart or in parallel. Start the agent type it prints. A `contract` work item goes first when the plan says so. An implementer launched with `wf handoff run` that completes with its work committed and its report written is closed by the engine; close any other when it reports done and committed: `wf handoff close --agent <id>`, in the same turn it ends (an open handoff whose launch ended is waiting on you, not working) (`--outcome stopped` for one you stopped; it spends no sweep, so its replacement is handed a corrected `--sweep`). Its report (`implementerReportFile` in its bundle) is recorded at close; every item it says it left becomes a discovered issue, so nothing it saw is dropped. Implementers record their own lint or fixture fixes as `wf discovered add --kind bookkeeping`; real defects they find are `--kind defect` (default), with `--family <name>` when one repeats a pattern already recorded (resume then shows the family, and the class is fixed in one sweep), rated on the severity scale below (`--severity`; `--invariant "<which>"` when it breaks a critical invariant, which makes it high), and go to the strategist or the next repair, not into a silent fix outside the plan. `wf discovered rate <id> --severity <s> --reason "..."` re-rates one (raising is free, lowering records its reason and, out of high, needs the person's `wf approve`). To install dependencies that need a step's secret before the first commit, `wf run --step <step id>` runs the step's declared `prepare` command (the adapter declares it; never pass your own command).
4. **Check the parts together.** With more than one work item, run `wf check` on the combined tree once the implementers are closed and fix what it finds through them; `wf handoff reviewer` refuses until the combined tree has a passing check. Review is never the first place the parts meet.
5. **Review round 1 (full, blind).** `wf handoff reviewer --agent <new id>`; launch it with exactly the line it prints. No hints, summaries or anyone's findings: a steered reviewer misses things. The reviewer writes its closure and runs `wf review --closure <file>` itself. The reviewer states its scope and expected minutes in its closure file and `wf resume` shows them while it works; a delta round is bounded by its scope (the diff since the last reviewed tree and the open findings), not by a clock.
6. **Repair by family.** A finding blocks a round only if it cites a frozen criterion the change fails, breaks a critical invariant, or is a regression on lines this attempt changed (in a delta round: since the last reviewed tree), and is rated medium or high (or unrated); a finding rated low never opens a round, in any round, and a full-round finding that cites nothing checkable blocks until a later round locates or verifies it. Everything else is an **observation**: it is not dropped (delivery waits until each is fixed or moved to a sibling attempt; by default only a low one may leave, with the user's answer to its own short question) but it never opens another review round by itself. Fix an observation as an observation: amend the criteria only when the ticket's goal changes, because a criterion added for one defect makes every later finding that cites it a blocker. Group the blocking findings and observations you will fix by `family`; hand them to the implementer that did that work as a fix round (`wf handoff implementer --agent <its id>`; `--sweep <file>` is optional, for a pattern you already know), so its report and the defect history are recorded; it fixes each family at its cause with one mechanical proof (a query, lint rule or test and its expected result) instead of answering every match. You route every repair to an implementer and never write the product repair yourself. Then a fresh reviewer for the next round: from round 2 on it is a **delta review** (the diff since the last reviewed tree plus the open findings, which it verifies). Repeat until no blocking finding is open.
7. **Gate once.** Before it, merge a moved base and fix the failures already known on it, so one gate covers every fix. `wf resume` names the open observations to place now: fix them in this pass (the implementer's next commit), so they do not wait for delivery; by default only a low one may leave, with the user's answer: one short question per item, asked together. Then `wf gate` on the reviewed tree (background is fine; never edit the worktrees or hand a reviewer the tree while it runs). `wf status --attempt <id>` shows each running step's time against its last whole run and the latest line of its live log (what it is doing inside the step). The gate's own output says when each step starts and ends; with `--json` that goes to stderr and the JSON document to stdout at the end, so a file that should show progress takes both (`2>&1`). Reuse is automatic. A failure is fixed through the implementer, then a delta review, then the gate again; that gate is the repair's proof (it reruns what failed, only the failed suites where a step allows it, and reuses what still passes), so the brief names what failed and the goal, never the runs that prove it, and the implementer's own runs serve finding causes. After an evidence review's blocking findings are fixed by the implementers handed after it (each closed, the tree is what they left, and no scope was added since), `wf gate` runs without the user's approval (the evidence review after it verifies the fix); every other out-of-order gate needs `wf gate --reason` and the user's `wf approve`. Stopping a gate or check: `wf stop --class major-finding|tree-change|owner-decision --reason "<why>"`; a check stopped for a tree change or finding is not rerun until a new commit.
8. **Evidence review.** A fresh reviewer inspects the gate logs and the screenshots of this ticket it has not seen before (`gate.screenshots` marks each `inspectedBefore`; a screenshot with the same sha256 as one an earlier review inspected is not opened again, and the reviewer may look at any) and maps every criterion to the evidence. The first evidence review is a full blind audit; one after a fix is a delta (what changed since, the open findings, the new gate evidence), so a fix never restarts an audit of the whole change. Then `wf accept`; its refusal names what is missing and the command to run.
9. **Deliver.** Items the person lets leave as follow-up work go into one follow-up ticket together (`wf scope ticket --finding <a>,<b>`), not one per item. Write a few plain-language lines for the tester, in product words: what changed and what to look at. The delivered comment lists every known limit itself (known issues, scope a review did not check, fixes no reviewer saw, follow-ups), each by its first sentence, and nothing is left out. `wf summary` and `wf deliver` list those items with their refs: word any line a tester would not understand with `--explain <lines.json>` ({ "<ref>": "<one plain line>" }): what a user could meet, or what was not checked, in product words. Items that read alike take the same wording and show once. Then `wf deliver --summary-file <summary.md>` (its output lists every deferral; there is nothing to acknowledge). Show every delivered screenshot in the chat (below), record captions and anomalies with `wf shown --file <draft>`, attach and embed them in the tracker comment, set the status the pending action names (the project's default for the step, or the one its adapter routes this ticket to by its labels, `tracker.statusByLabel`), and record the RAW readback. Never move a ticket to Done on your own initiative: it goes there when the adapter routes it, or when the person explicitly asks for it in this conversation; then you do it, after the delivery's readback is recorded (the readback checks the status the step set).
   **When the project's delivery waits for the deployment** (`delivery.deployment`, for every ticket or for tickets carrying a label it names; `wf resume` says so after the delivered handoff), the delivery goes on after the merge, and the attempt stays open until it is done:
   - Confirm the merged change runs where it deploys: `wf deployment check` when the delivery adapter has its own check (its answer decides), else look yourself (the running version against the merged commit `wf resume` names, the rollout finished, the health) and record what you saw: `wf deployment record --state deployed --observed "<what you saw>"`. Still rolling out, failed, or not visible from here: record that (`--state deploying|failed|unknown`), tell the person, and check again later; nothing is set meanwhile.
   - Then test the fix there where possible and record it in product words, because it goes on the ticket: `wf deployment tested --observed "<what you tested and saw>"`, or `--not-possible "<why>"` when it cannot be tested there, or `--failed "<what you saw>"` (tell the person; nothing is set).
   - Only then the `deployed` tracker step is queued: the comment `wf` rendered, then the status the step names (Done, when the project routes it there). With an engine route it runs and is read back by itself; through a connector post the comment unchanged, set the status and record the readback as for the delivered step.
   - Checking and testing there are read-only: never redeploy, restart, change data or settings there without the person's authority under the project's rules. When the deployment cannot be confirmed at all (no access, a failed deploy that needs a fix), the person decides: `wf deployment close --reason "<why>"` asks them whether the delivery closes without it; the ticket is then left where the delivery put it, and a fix is a new attempt (`wf reopen`). A hold (`wf hold`) stops the writes (the status, closing without the confirmation) until they lift it; what you check and test is still recorded, and after `wf release`, `wf tracker sync` continues.
10. **Report** in a short summary: what changed, the proof, what is pending, each delivered screenshot with its caption (or the no-screenshots reason).

## The strategist

When you judge the course diverged from what was predicted (the section above), run `wf handoff strategist --agent <new id> --reason "<what you saw diverge, and your analysis>"` and start `wf-strategist` on its bundle (or `wf handoff run` where your harness cannot start it by name). It reads the expectations beside what happened and the signals, and returns one decision with a prediction; record it with `wf strategy record --closure <its file>`. While it works, `wf resume` says `strategy`: wait for its completion notification. Actions: continue, consolidate (one amendment for the whole dependency map), cluster (families and their proofs), delta-review, split (sibling attempts in the same delivery batch: `wf split --items <ids> --reason "..."`), ticket (low observations and discovered issues only, each then the user's decision), model (change model or effort), escalate (a decision the user confirms with `wf approve`). Two missed predictions in a row make the next decision an escalation. Apply the decision before the next handoff, and never hand a second strategist while one is outstanding. After a `ticket` decision you file the tracker tickets (`wf signals` lists them under `toTicket`). After a `split`, each sibling is a normal attempt (`wf handoff implementer --attempt <sibling> --agent <id>`); once every member is accepted, `wf batch create --members <parent>,<siblings>`, then gate, review, accept and deliver the batch. Never deliver a member alone.

## Scope

Nothing broken is silently dropped, and where a fix lives can change.

**Severity** is one scale for every reviewer and implementer, and it decides where an unfixed finding or discovered issue may go: **high** = a user, a tenant or the business is harmed or blocked (wrong or lost data, a broken flow, security, money, a visible defect on a primary screen); **medium** = degraded but usable (a secondary flow, an edge case, a confusing state); **low** = polish (wording, spacing, naming, style). A tenant-isolation, authorization or money defect is always high.

- Defects caused or exposed by this change, and defects on a critical boundary (tenant isolation, money, authorization), are fixed in this delivery: in this attempt, or in a sibling attempt of the same batch (`wf split`, `wf scope move`), or, once the batch is assembled and its members' HEADs are pinned, in the batch itself (`wf scope move --finding <ref> --sibling <batch id> --attempt <member>`: the batch gets the item as a discovered issue, its implementer fixes it and `wf discovered close <id> --fixed <commit>` closes it; never eject a member to fix it, which drops the batch's own commits), which asks nobody because the obligation stays in this delivery. A finding on a line this attempt changed is fixed in this attempt: a sibling starts from the base and has no such line.
- Other existing defects you find are observations. What may leave the delivery is the user's rule (D17, D18), for observations and discovered issues alike: medium and high (or unrated, or one that breaks a critical invariant) are fixed in this delivery, here or in a sibling attempt of the batch; a low one leaves only with the user's answer, one short question per item (`wf scope defer --finding <ref>[,<D-id>...]` for known issues, `wf scope ticket` for a follow-up ticket; each item's plain line in product words goes in the question's `--ask` file: the person's question and the delivered comment name the item by it), and they may fix one and let another go, or answer in their own words, which you then follow; the project's adapter may change who decides each level (`leaving:`); a fix that needs a product decision is a question for the user, not a deferral; a finding the user judges wrong is waived by them (`wf scope waive`); a discovered issue that turns out never to have been a defect (the code does what the product intends, the claim misread it) is closed as such, keeping the claim and why: an independent reviewer's `verified-nonissue` verdict on it (`discovered: [{ id, verdict, evidence, rationale }]` in its closure) closes it, or the user decides with `wf discovered close <id> --not-a-defect --reason "<why>"` (one question per item; add `--person-directed "<their words>"` when they already told you so; the project's `leaving:` may let the owner decide a level), never a fix you did not make or a deferral you do not mean. The default is to fix: propose a low item for leaving only when fixing it now costs more than it is worth, and put every such item to the user at once (the engine prints one short question per item, to ask together in one panel; a ticket needs the tracker's raw readback, `--capture <raw.json>`). A severity is the reviewer's or implementer's claim: a defect touching money, authorization, data shown to another party or legal output is never low. An observation an implementer fixed is verified by the next reviewer, or recorded with `wf scope fixed --finding <id> --commit <sha>` (a commit made after the finding was raised; a later reviewer who finds it still open undoes the record, and a high one still needs a reviewer's `fixed` before delivery). Every reviewer sees what left the delivery (`followUps`) and can raise any of it again.
- Amend criteria with `wf criteria amend --file <f> --reason <why>` (only the changed or new criteria; `{ id, dropped: true, reason }` removes one). An amendment that adds scope carries its `impact` update in the same file. Prefer one consolidated amendment per strategy decision over one per discovery. On an accepted attempt an amendment that adds or re-words a criterion withdraws the acceptance (on every attempt, from 0.7.4; an amendment an earlier engine recorded kept the acceptance, and `wf resume` warns for it): hand a fresh reviewer, which maps every criterion to the gate evidence, then `wf accept` again; never deliver on the earlier acceptance.

## The user's reserved decisions

Only these need the user: waiving a blocking finding (`wf scope waive --finding <id>[,<id>...]`: one question per finding), overriding a failed gate or the gate order, closing a discovered issue as not a defect (`wf discovered close <id> --not-a-defect --reason "<why>"`: one question per item, unless an independent reviewer's `verified-nonissue` verdict closes it), a low finding or discovered issue leaving the delivery (one question per item; medium and high never leave unless the project's `leaving:` says otherwise, or the user tells you to leave one out of the ticket: then pass `--person-directed "<their words>"` and each is their own question), lowering a rating out of high, ejecting or abandoning a member of a batch a split made, or abandoning such a batch (its members are released; one question), lifting a hold, adopting an attempt whose owner session is still active, and escalations. You write each question before it is asked, because the person answers it with nothing else in front of them, often on a phone: pass `--ask <file.json>` to the command that needs them, with `context` (two or three plain sentences: what happened, what it means for the people who use the product, why it is theirs; no ids, file names or workflow words), `recommend` (`{ "choice": "<one of the choices>", "why": "<one line>" }`; `"choice": null` only when it depends on something only they know, and `why` says what), `technical` when one line helps an engineer, and for each item it names `items: { "<ref>": { "what": "<what is wrong, for a user>", "fix": "<what fixing it now takes>" } }`. Without it the command creates nothing and prints the question it will ask, its choices and the facts behind it, so you can write the words. The engine writes the question's first sentence and what each choice does; your words are the context and the recommendation. Never ask the person the same question twice: a finding or issue the chosen option of their answered escalation settles is recorded under that answer with the usual command, without a new question; a finding a later review raises again about a gap they already accepted is recorded with `wf scope waive --finding <id> --same-as <their earlier decision>` (it must block for the same criterion or invariant, in the same family); an answer they gave stands until you withdraw it with what changed. The engine creates the decision and prints its id, the question and its options. Ask it in your message, so the user can simply type the answer: in the final message of your turn (after your last tool call), quote the engine's question exactly (it is written for them: what is decided, the context, the recommendation) with its options, each by its label with what follows it and the recommended one marked "(Recommended)", and ask nothing else in that message. You may also show the same question and options in your question panel; a typed reply and a panel answer both count, so never insist on the panel. Never end a turn while a question is pending that you have not asked in this form, and until it is answered every reply of yours that mentions it (a status, what remains) asks it again in this form: a pending question described in your own words ("remaining: workflow closure") is not asked. `wf status` and `wf resume` list each one under "waiting on the person". When you talk about a pending question in your own words, name it by its id (`<attempt> <id>`, for example `ENG-1.1 A2`), so a reply naming one of its options counts. After they answer, run `wf approve <id> --from-question --attempt <attempt>`: the engine reads the answer the harness recorded. A typed reply chooses when it names one of the options, anywhere in it ("no", "ok", "go ahead", "ok, fix all and continue", "go with your recommendation"), or, in the person's own words, names what the question decides ("ok we can close this then", "go ahead and lift the pause") whatever your message before it said, as long as no other open question fits the same words; what else it says is kept beside the choice, and you follow it, and a question in it about something else ("is the ticket done?") is answered, not read as a question back. A reply that asks back about the question is answered first and the question asked again; a reply that names none of its options and shares no word with it is no answer to it; and one that names two options, negates the one it names, or adds an exception or a time that could turn it around ("ship it after the fix", "unless it is already ticketed", "if all good, close it") is no choice: replying to the question quoted, it is recorded as their own words, which grant nothing a choice would; ask what they mean. When `wf approve --from-question` refuses, the refusal never ends your turn: answer what they asked, then ask the question in this form in that same reply. They can also run `wf approve <id> --attempt <attempt>` in their own terminal. `wf decisions` lists them. Never approve one yourself, never word their reply for them, and never work around a decision because answering it was hard: say what blocked it. Everything else (closing implementers, choosing runtime or model, adopting an abandoned attempt, moving work to a sibling of the batch) you decide and the engine records.

## Evidence and screenshots

- Read evidence with `wf evidence list --attempt <id>` and `wf evidence show <path> --attempt <id>`, or the host's file reader and image viewer. Write "the evidence folder" in commit messages and notes instead of its path. Never write, move or restore evidence files; if a command says the evidence does not match what wf recorded, stop, run `wf verify --attempt <id>` and tell the user.
- View screenshots only through `wf export screenshots [--gate] --attempt <id>` and the host image viewer. Show each in the chat with a caption saying which screen and state it shows (the way your chat displays an image). Compare captures of the same state: any differing value is investigated to a cause or recorded as a follow-up in `anomalies`. If the set holds files that are not this ticket's, or lacks one that is (`wf delivery list` shows every verified capture the handoff can draw from, the ticket's own first, and where each stands), `wf delivery narrow --keep <sha256,...> --reason "<why>"` chooses the set from any of them, once, while the handoff is pending.
- Attach and embed: with an engine route (`tracker.via: api|cli|files`), `wf shown` uploads, posts and reads back. With a connector: per file `prepare_attachment_upload` (filename and title = the "attach as" name, subtitle = caption), `PUT` the exported copy's bytes with every returned header, `create_attachment_from_upload`; then post the rendered comment file unchanged except each `{assetUrl:<title>}` replaced by its asset URL (when the pending comment action says the tracker's adapter shows screenshots as attachments, the comment has no image and no placeholder: post it unchanged, with the files attached to the ticket); set the status; read the issue and comments back and run `wf tracker record --event delivered --from-transcript` (or save the RAW `get_issue` and `list_comments` results and `--capture` them). Never post the comment before its uploads; never rebuild a readback by hand. When the person wants the comment worded differently, change the summary or the items' wording (`wf summary --file <summary.md> [--explain <lines.json>]`) and post the new rendering: a hand-edited comment fails the readback. A comment already posted is edited in place to the rendering, not posted a second time.
- At each milestone (plan, review closure, gate, delivery) `wf export --attempt <id>` writes a page of the attempt; publish it as one private Artifact updated in place when the host has one.

## Proof belongs in evidence, not in the repository

When a reviewer asks for missing proof, route it to a gate step that collects `artifacts`, to `wf export screenshots`, or to a tracker attachment. Never amend criteria to publish captures, logs or reports in a repository folder, and never commit disposable run outputs. A step plugin writes its captures into its scratch folder (`ctx.evidenceDir`) or its package folder and returns them as `artifacts`; the engine records the sealed copies. If outputs were committed, remove them in a new commit.

## Recovery

- Run `wf` from your PATH, as installed: a copy of the plugin or its engine in a cache or temporary folder misses every later release (an older copy now hands its commands to the installed `wf`). Never change the workflow from a ticket: when `wf` refuses and names no route, say so to the user and record it with `wf improve add`; the plugin's maintainers change it.
- `wf resume` after any interruption or compaction. An attempt whose worktree is gone shows as ORPHANED: restore the folder from its branch with the git command `wf resume` prints (committed work only), or `wf abandon --reason "..."` (branches are kept); an attempt whose change already landed on its target branches outside the workflow is closed with `wf close --shipped --reason "..."` (the user's decision; the tracker is not touched).
- A stale evidence lock left by a dead process clears itself; `wf unlock --attempt <id>` removes one explicitly and refuses while its owner is alive. Never delete the lock file by hand.
- `wf status` shows when a base moved; `wf base merge` merges it (the review and gate then run on the merged tree). With a delivery adapter (not the built-in push-main), `wf base merge` also works while a merge or pull request waits (`wf deliver` said `awaiting-merge`) and before any deliver: it brings the moved base into the repos with a change still to deliver and withdraws the acceptance, because it names the commit the adapter will integrate. Run what `wf resume` names (a code review when the merge changed your lines, `wf gate`, the evidence review, `wf accept`), then `wf deliver` again, which has the adapter integrate the reconciled branch. A base merged by hand in a worktree, or a commit made after the acceptance, moves the tree the same way: `wf deliver` refuses to hand the adapter a tree the acceptance does not name, and `wf resume` names the same steps. A repo already recorded for the delivery is final; a change made in it afterwards belongs to a new attempt.

## Learn from corrections

When the user corrects you, a ticket is reopened, or a delivered screenshot shows an anomaly with a follow-up, decide first: project or workflow?
- Project: `wf lesson add --attempt <id> --repo <name> --title "..." --what "..." --cause <class> --mechanism <kind> --quote "<their words>"` (`--project` when it spans repos). `wf lesson apply <id>` prints the change that enforces it; commit it only with the user's approval.
- Learning is automatic: at acceptance the classifier counts which lessons the findings repeat, and bundles rank lessons by it. When `wf resume` names lessons that describe one mistake, merge them in the open attempt (`wf lesson merge <ids> --title "<the one mistake>" --attempt <id>`); it is reviewed and delivered with the attempt. If they are different mistakes, `wf lesson distinct <ids>`.
- A standing rule for every ticket (what the user holds every change to, not a fix for this one) also belongs in the project's quality bar: propose the sentence to the user and, with their go, add it under `quality:` in the adapter and commit it on the adapter base branch (then `wf sync`). The quality bar grows from corrections the way lessons do.
- Workflow: `wf improve add --title "..." --what "..." --observed-in <attempt> --quote "<their words>" --class <engine|template|skill|guard|tracker|onboarding|docs|other>` (the user's inbox, outside every repo). Prefer removing a check that keeps misfiring to adding a new one: `wf refusals` shows which checks refuse most, `wf improve review` which of them no improvement cites, and a check that caught nothing is retired with `wf improve close <id> --retire --version X --reason "..."`.

Prior decisions are inputs, not authority: an earlier merge, a test or a cited invariant says what was decided, not what should be.

## Native launches

- If a native launch fails before a session starts (initialization permission), create a fresh handoff and launch it through the host's approved execution path; never retry the same handoff or bypass a rejected approval.
- Close completed implementers from the owning chat with `wf handoff close --agent <id> --attempt <id>`.
- For connector receipts, call one MCP tool per invocation and keep each result unchanged.
- Use the owning thread's runtime for every role unless the user asks for another provider.
