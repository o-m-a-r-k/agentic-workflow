# The way of working

The goal: a ticket goes in, and the outcome it exists for comes true for its users, right and as soon as it can be done right, with no impact nobody saw coming. Nothing wrong reaches them, above all the facts they act on. The person sees the plan, and is asked once per ticket, in one message, for the product decisions and, when the risk is high, approval of the outcome and its Surface.

## The outcome

Everything else in this method serves this section.

- **Every ticket is for an outcome:** what is true in its users' world once it is delivered, not the code, the tests or the steps: the failing request now completes for the customer who hit it; the new report gives the totals its readers act on. Write down the outcome, its most direct evidence (the users' own data and logs, read-only; the screen as they meet it; the money the feature produces) and how soon it is needed.
- **The orchestrator owns the distance to it.** The next move is the one that most shortens it, or removes the largest uncertainty about it, for its cost. The steps below are the usual moves in their usual order; the outcome decides how deep each goes, never that the plan judge, review, verification, a high-risk approval or the delivery links are skipped.
- **Direct evidence first; stand-ins only while they track it.** A local reproduction, a mocked state, a passing suite, a reader's answers and a judge's verdict stand in for the outcome's own evidence, and count only while they move with it. When one doesn't, the work goes back to the outcome's evidence; making the stand-in stricter is not progress.
- **A move earns its place by what it could still reveal or change about the outcome,** weighed against the time it costs the people waiting. An unlikely, cheap failure is an accepted risk with its numbers. A wrong fact people act on, or wrong data or state, once shown reachable through the product's real paths (its users, providers and jobs), however rare, is never an accepted risk: a rare failure on a path that writes such a fact is made to stop safely (stuck, visible, correctable by an operator), and automatic recovery is a decision memo with its cost.
- **Progress is the distance shrinking,** judged against when the outcome is needed. A finding, a revision or a passing run is not progress by itself.
- **The users' part ships first.** The plan names the smallest change that makes the outcome true for the users waiting, proven and leaving nothing wrong reachable, and shows it can be delivered alone or says why not. When the related work beyond it is large, it follows as this ticket's next delivery. A proven users' part is held back only by a regression the change caused or by what it can't be true without.

## The Surface

A change shows or writes facts people act on: an amount, an appointment time, a slot's availability, a status, a balance, who may do what. Each must equal its record (the stored state, a provider's, or the rule that derives it from them) in every reachable state. The Surface lists, one line each, every such fact the change shows or writes: its kind, where it is met, and the record it must equal. The project definition names its kinds and their oracles. It is drafted when the ticket is taken (`STATE.md`), possibly empty, and finalised in the plan; a draft line the plan drops is marked "not touched" with its source, and the plan judge judges that claim like any other. Each line has a check in `CHECKS.json` whose `expect` would fail on the wrong state, comparing what is shown or written with the record read apart from the code that shows it: the oracle for its kind where the project has one (run from the project definition's folder, never from the branch under test), otherwise a read-only query or a walk. The Surface is what the person approves on a high-risk ticket, what review item 4 and verification prove, and what the cold audit compares after delivery.

Approval is asked again only when a Surface line is added or removed, turns touched or not touched, or changes its kind or the record it must equal; wording is not a change. `plan-shape` prints the hash of those, the approval records it, and `links` compares them. Nothing on the Surface, and nothing the outcome names, is ever dropped, limited, cut or delivered as a stated limit; no verdict or question form in this method offers that.

## How the work is trusted

- **Agents judge meaning; scripts count, match and compare.** No script decides what the work is. The six in the skill folder's `scripts/` (`plan-shape`, `rounds`, `proof`, `checks`, `links`, `record`) run as `node <skill folder>/scripts/<name>.mjs`; each reads the ticket folder and git, writes one result file in `evidence/`, exits 0 or 1, and says at its top what it compares. The role files say who runs which, and when. A `checks` run is its ticket's alone: `evidence/checks.run` names it and `checks <folder> --stop` ends it, or clears what a killed run left; the machine is shared, so wait or stop by that, never by matching process names.
- **Agents check agents.** A fresh agent checks the work against raw sources: code, git, logs, data, the running app; confidence never comes from the author's own account. A judge answers each rubric item yes or no with the evidence that decided it; an item without evidence is no. A judge runs once per version of its object, on a different model or effort than the author where the harness allows, and never again on the same version.
- **Grounded.** Every claim names its source: `file:line` at a named commit, a read-only query and its result, a log line, a screenshot, the ticket, or an authoritative external source with its date. A decision names the alternative it beats.
- **Findings are claims too.** Whoever receives one checks it first, and disputes a false one with evidence; the next judge settles it. A finding counts by what it does to the outcome or to what already works; taste is not a finding.
- **Prove by running.** A question the design hinges on is settled by running something. Whoever writes a test runs it, through `proof`. Whatever the project's environments can produce (a state, an account, data, a running app) is produced and checked, never reported as unchecked or a limit; only what can't be produced there, such as a real third party, real money or elapsed time, is reported, first, as work not done, with why.
- **Proof belongs to a version.** The judge and the approval name the plan revision, tests and review the commit and its tree, verification the build. When what was proven changes, the proof that rested on it no longer counts and is done again for what changed. A base update proves again only what it changed in what this change alters or relies on.
- **Meaning first.** What a person sees exists so they understand and can act; the plugin's `design` skill holds how.
- **Files, not memory.** The ticket folder is the state. Where the files and anyone's recollection disagree, the files win.

## Scope

The outcome as its users meet it, whatever they can't get it without and whatever the change caused or broke are the ticket's, fixed in it at their root. Everything else found on the way is extra, recorded under "Outside the ticket" in `STATE.md`. An extra is a finding like any other: it names the state that triggers it and shows it by running it (a failing test, a query, the screen), the same as a review finding. One that can't be shown in the project's environments is a lead: it goes into the handoff with what would show it, and opens no ticket.

- **A Surface extra,** a wrong fact people act on, shown reachable by users now, opens a hotfix ticket at once and the person is told at once; it starts after the current delivery unless they say now, and they can veto it. Where the tracker refuses the ticket, it is written in `STATE.md` and the handoff.
- **A change to an oracle** is the ticket's when its outcome needs it; any other is its own ticket.
- **Every other extra** goes into the handoff, the Surface's kinds first, each in plain words (what is wrong, who meets it, what fixing it would take) with a recommendation to file it, fix it now or after this delivery.

The project's `AGENTS.md` files and the person's own instructions add to this method; where they ask for more, they win. They never remove a step: authority to deliver doesn't approve a high-risk plan. Only the person, of their own accord in the session, can waive a step, for the ticket they name; never ask them to.

## Ticket types and size

The type is one of four. The person names it in the session, unless the project definition says where it comes from; otherwise infer it and record that it was inferred.

- **Hotfix:** the people hitting a production failure work again, now, and its cause is removed at its root; nothing else is its outcome. Getting them working again is its users' part; where the cause can still reach users it is guarded to fail safe. Removing the cause follows as the next delivery when it takes longer.
- **Bug:** the failing behaviour works again as it did, or as it was meant to, with its cause removed.
- **Enhancement:** behaviour that exists changes as asked, and everything that relies on it keeps working.
- **New feature:** its users can do the new thing, correctly, in every real state.

A small ticket uses few agents: a backend bug or hotfix is investigated and planned by one worker and has no reader, and the worker that delivers it records and cleans: seven agents with one review and the audit. Whatever the size, the one who checks is never the one who wrote, every plan is judged and every change is reviewed and verified.

## The steps

1. **Take.** Choose the delivery target as the project definition says (a hotfix's is the one production is built from). Create `<work>/<ticket>/` from the templates and a worktree per repo the ticket needs, each from its freshly fetched base and set up by a worker; a consumer repo found later gets its worktree then. If the work folder lies inside a repo, its `.git/info/exclude` lists it. Move the ticket to `started`. Fill `STATE.md`: the outcome, the type, the worktrees and a first draft of the Surface. Tell the person in one line the outcome, type, size and the moves you expect.

2. **Understand.** One to three investigators, one angle each. A bug or hotfix reproduces first: the failure seen where it happens (logs, traces, read-only data, the screen as its user sees it), or a failing test; anything that would write there is reproduced locally with data shaped like production. Walk the users' path, read-only, before planning; ask the person at once for any access that needs. Find the reach: what uses what the change alters, through code, shared data, events or contracts, inside the worktrees or beyond them, and what elsewhere does the same job. One pass per angle; what isn't settled is listed open, never sent to a second investigator.

3. **Plan.** `PLAN.md` and `CHECKS.json`, in the templates' form: the decisions, each with its choice, source and the alternative it beats; the tests table, each row with whether it fails before the change and the delivery it is due by; the Surface finalised, with a runnable check per line; the reach, each part covered by a test or check; the deliveries, the users' part first; any product memos. No narrative. The planner runs `plan-shape`, fixes what fails once, and what still fails stands in `evidence/plan-shape.md` as unsourced for the judge. A planner that needs investigation asks once; after that the plan goes ahead with its open questions listed. A changed decision raises the revision and rewrites the plan in place; there are no per-revision copies.

4. **Judge the plan.** One fresh judge per plan revision, `rounds` first, answers the plan rubric (`roles/judge.md`): `build`, `fix items … once` or `approval needed`. The planner folds the items in place, keeping the revision, and the build starts. A second judge comes only after an implementer returns `plan wrong`: the orchestrator moves the judge file aside as `judge-<n>-r<revision>.md`, the planner revises, and a fresh judge reads the new revision. A third `plan wrong` holds the ticket, and the person gets one message with the evidence. A later delivery on an unchanged plan needs no new judge.

5. **The person, once.** One message per ticket carries every product memo with its recommendation and, when the risk is high, the outcome and the Surface to approve, linking the plan. Record their words, the time, the revision and the Surface hash in `STATE.md`. Ask again only when a Surface line changes.

6. **Build.** Fresh implementers take the plan as their brief, lanes for independent items, each in its own worktree; the implementer of the last lane merges them in order. Each tests row is run through `proof`, one command naming every row it proves: red on the base tree with only the test applied, where it fails before, and green on HEAD with the same words. A green holds only for the tree it ran on; a row `links` refuses comes with its reason and the words to run. The commands the project definition says every change runs, run too. A new fact that changes a decision goes into the plan; one that raises the risk or changes a Surface line goes to the person; a plan wrong at its root returns `plan wrong`. Done is `done at <sha>`.

7. **Read** (screens only). One fresh reader per delivery, on the built screenshots (the orchestrator runs `rounds` for it, since it sees nothing else), answers the purpose's five to eight questions for each pictured state: yes or no, where on the screen, from an element or only from a sentence (`roles/read.md`). Its "no"s go to review. A reader that returns blocked hasn't spent its round. Screens changed after the read are reported by `links` in the handoff, not read again.

8. **Review.** A fresh judge reads the whole change against the review rubric (`roles/review.md`): at most two rounds per delivery, `rounds` first each time, one implementer between them, round 2 reading the fixes. After round 2 the implementer fixes, with a test, what the plan's Outcome names and is still open, and says which those are. A third, narrow review reads only what is left: the fix of an open `[S<n>]` finding (one on a Surface line), or any diff after review that `links` reports (a defect verification found, a base conflict, a fix outside the findings' files); its findings all count as Surface findings. After it, an open finding or a further change holds the delivery, and the handoff says so. Everything else goes into the handoff as findings; nothing is dropped and nobody is asked. A review file whose verdict and findings disagree is rewritten in place by a fresh reviewer; it is not a round.

9. **Verify** on the final tree, in the target environment as the project defines it (`roles/verify.md`): each check as written, `checks` for the runnable ones, the walks through the product's real paths, screenshots of the named states, and the tests the reach covers. Every check due is true with an evidence file naming HEAD. A check that fails on that commit is this ticket's to fix, as a diff after review, unless it fails the same way on the base and the outcome doesn't need it, which is an extra.

10. **Deliver.** `links` first: a broken link stops the delivery and names the worker to start; a held delivery still gets its handoff. Then a fresh delivery worker (`roles/deliver.md`) brings the branch up to date where needed, takes each irreversible step after `links` holds at that commit, confirms each from its output, and looks for the outcome where it lives. The handoff opens with the outcome and the evidence it is now true, pastes the `links` table, attaches the screenshots where the definition says, and lists the extras. A remaining delivery that can only wait for what nobody in the session can produce (an outside party's answer, elapsed time; never the person's own memo or approval, which are asked) doesn't keep the ticket open: once every other delivery is delivered, the delivery worker opens a follow-up ticket carrying that delivery's plan, its branch if built (clean leaves it) and what it waits for, the handoff lists it first as work not done, and the ticket moves to its delivered status. If nothing is delivered yet, the ticket is held and the person told. Where the tracker refuses the follow-up, it goes in `STATE.md` and the handoff.

11. **Cold audit.** Right after each delivery, a hotfix's guard delivery included, one fresh agent compares every fact on the Surface with its record, through the oracle for its kind (`roles/audit.md`). A kind with no oracle is named in the handoff as checked only by walk; a confirmed wrong fact opens a hotfix as Scope says. `links` for the next delivery and `record` report a delivered delivery with no audit.

12. **Record and clean,** after the last delivery and its audit, by the auditor: `evidence/missed.md`, `record`, and removing what the ticket left (`roles/deliver.md`), the ticket folder last.

Rounds per delivery: one judge per revision, one reader, two reviews and the narrow third, counted by `rounds` from the exact evidence file names (`judge-<n>.md`, `read-<n>.md`, `review-<n>-<round>.md`). A judge past its cap refuses itself; an overrun is reported in the `links` table, the handoff and the record.

## Several tickets

Each ticket is independent and is delivered when it is ready. Nothing waits for a shared gate, and a pause for one ticket never pauses another. A heavy test run starts when there is room for it by measured memory, as the project definition says; otherwise it waits, never skipped. `heavy_runs`, where the settings set it, is an outer limit. The most urgent outcome goes first.

## Learning

The records, their `missed` lists, the cold audits and the reopened tickets show where the method fails. A kind of miss that repeats points at the method: find what let the work drift from the outcome and change that, rather than adding a clause for the case. A seventh script needs a failure class the records show was missed twice. Learning is its own piece of work, never done inside a ticket or in the installed copy.
