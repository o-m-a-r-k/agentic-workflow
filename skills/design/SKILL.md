# Screens that do their job

**The goal.** A person looks at the screen and knows, without working anything out, where they are, what is true, what matters most and what to do next, and can do it without fear of the wrong move. It looks like it belongs beside the product's best screens, and an experienced product designer would not change its structure; the passes under Practice are how that is judged. Fitting every width, following the design system and answering every question are part of that bar, not the bar.

## Principles

- **Purpose before layout.** Start from who uses it, in what situation, what they must come away knowing or doing, and the questions they bring, in the order they bring them. That chooses what appears, in what order and in what form. How the data is stored or returned is never the layout: a screen that mirrors its payload, one field or one item per row, has skipped design.
- **Importance is visible.** Each view has one focal point: the answer the person came for, and the next step. Size, weight, colour, position and space rank everything else beneath it. Things that look equally strong are read as equally important, so rank is designed, never left to the order the parts were built in.
- **Group by meaning, separate by space.** What belongs together sits together under one name; what doesn't stands apart. Space does this before lines and boxes do. A group's name says what its members have in common.
- **Like things look alike.** Items of the same kind get the same component, alignment, order and treatment everywhere on the screen. A different look means a different meaning; a difference with no meaning is noise.
- **Craft is part of meaning.** A few type sizes and weights, each with one job. Everything aligns to shared edges, and actions of one kind sit in one place. Spacing comes from the system's scale, with more space between groups than within them. Density fits the task: a tool used all day is compact and scannable, a rare task roomy and guided.
- **One clear next step.** The primary action is one, and it is the step the person most likely needs. Other actions are quieter and grouped together. Emphasis and colour rank actions and carry meaning, never decoration: danger styling only for what is destructive or irreversible, and where the brand colour reads as danger, secondary actions are neutral. What can't be done now appears only when knowing it changes what the person does, and then reads as information, not as a control.
- **What it says and what it allows agree.** A warning, a status and the actions offered beside them tell one story in every state; a step the screen advises against is not presented as a ready choice.
- **Show the common, disclose the rare.** What most people need most of the time is on the surface; detail, technical facts and rare paths sit behind a clearly named disclosure. The answer to a main question is never hidden.
- **The person's words and formats.** Labels name what a thing means to them, not what the system calls it. One thing has one name everywhere, on the screen and across the product. A button names its outcome, not a generic verb. Values are formatted as the person would say them: money with its currency, dates in their calendar, states as words. Information leads; a sentence appears only where a fact alone would be misread. Every word earns its place.
- **Every action answers.** Its result shows where the person is looking, without covering what they need next. Prevent errors before explaining them. Confirm only what can't be undone, and say in the confirmation what will happen, to what, for how much; prefer undo. An error says what happened and how to put it right, next to its cause.
- **Every real state is designed.** Empty, loading, partial, failed, long values, many items, a limited role, each language and direction: each says what it means and what the person can do, in the same structure as the normal state.
- **It belongs to the product.** Use the design system's tokens and components and the patterns the product already uses for this kind of screen. A new pattern is a product decision, made only when none fits.
- **Everyone can use it, at every size.** Readable contrast, meaning never carried by colour alone, a sensible focus and reading order, full keyboard use, comfortable touch targets, layout that mirrors for right-to-left languages. At narrow widths the order follows importance: the answer and the primary action stay within reach, and the layout reflows rather than shrinks. Motion only shows continuity or a change of state, and respects reduced-motion settings.

## Practice

- **Prove it as a picture before building it.** A visible change is settled by rendering it before the build, from the project's real components with data shaped like real use, in a throwaway spike in the worktree, removed once its pictures are saved and never committed, using the cheapest renderer the project names; the pictures go to `evidence/`, named by direction, state and width. A small change gets one picture. A new screen, a contested one or one that failed its purpose gets two or three directions that differ in structure (the focal point, the groups, where actions live and how they rank), not in colour or spacing. A change that can't alter composition, such as a word or a token, is proven by the built screen's screenshot instead.
- **Picture what carries the decision.** The pictures show the focal point, the full set of actions and the groups, in the states that stress them: the most actions, the longest values, the narrowest width, the hardest real state. A direction that leaves one out has not been compared.
- **Have it read fresh.** Compare the directions against the purpose, these principles and the product's best screens of the same kind; then a fresh reader, given only the chosen picture and its purpose, answers the person's questions from it before anything is built. What it can't answer goes back into the design, not into the build.
- **Judge in passes, from the rendered screen.** First glance: what is seen first, and is it what the person most needs? Squint: does the hierarchy hold when the words blur? Read: can each of the person's questions be answered from the screen, and does everything on it tell one story? Detail: consistency, alignment, type, spacing, words and every real state. Judge against the product's best screens of the same kind and recognised practice for that kind of screen, never against the nearest page. Be strict: "acceptable" is not the bar, and a screenshot or the code is not the live screen.
- **A screen that fails its purpose is redesigned from its purpose,** as the deliver workflow's "When work stalls" describes.

The project supplies its specifics: its design system and where it lives, its components, its best reference screen for each kind of surface, its languages, themes and widths, and how to render a throwaway picture from real components. They come from its `AGENTS.md` and its project definition.
